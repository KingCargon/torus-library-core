# Open core

## What is open

Everything in this repository: the schema, the permanent identifier
scheme, Markdown-first records, metadata validation, the publication state
machine, PDF generation, integrity hashing, canon freeze, amendments,
superseding revisions, manifests, the chronicle engine, the audience
model, fail-closed public export, diligence primitives, the
restricted-reference schema, portable export packages, the citation
contract, the CLI, local and self-hosted operation, and public site
rendering.

That is the whole engine. It is not a demo, a trial, or a crippled tier.

**A company can run this and own its institutional memory outright** — on
its own disk, with no account, no service, and no dependency on whoever
wrote it. That is the point, and nothing is withheld to make it less true.

## What is not open

Not because the core would be better without them — because they are
different products:

- **Managed hosting** — running it for you.
- **Automation and integrations** — pulling records out of the systems a
  company already uses.
- **Team workflows** — review, approval, multi-author publishing.
- **Diligence workflows and services** — assembling a data room, not just
  indexing one.
- **Enterprise governance and security** — beyond what a file-based
  archive can express.
- **Implementation and support.**

And no organisation's own archive is open. The engine is the product; the
contents are yours.

## Why this split

An institutional archive is a strange thing to rent. Its whole value is
permanence, and permanence is exactly what you lose when your history
lives inside someone else's subscription.

So the part that must outlive us is open, and the paid work is the labour
of running and integrating it — which is genuinely labour, and which
nobody is forced to buy.

## In practice

Fork it. Run it forever. No key expires. Your records are Markdown, PDFs,
and a JSON manifest on your own disk. If this project stopped tomorrow,
your archive would still open in a text editor and a PDF reader.
